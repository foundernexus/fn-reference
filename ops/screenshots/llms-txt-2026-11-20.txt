# Founder Decisions

> Decision pages for venture-scale founders. Sourced decision pages for seed to Series B founders: fundraising, equity, hiring, the board, finance, and go-to-market. One question per page. Every figure on a page names its source. Pages are not legal, tax, or investment advice.

Each page answers one founder question in its first paragraph, then shows the cited tables, steps, and sources behind the answer.

## Topics

- [Library](https://founderdecisions.com/library/): Sourced decision pages for seed to Series B founders: fundraising, equity, hiring, the board, finance, and go-to-market. One question per page.
- [Equity & cap table](https://founderdecisions.com/library/equity/): Size the option pool through the next round, treat each grant as a pool draw, and know who takes the dilution when the pool tops up.

- [Go-to-market](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/): Twenty-two go-to-market decisions for seed to Series B founders, grouped by stage: validation, first deals, pricing, retention, and reach.

## Top pages

- [How much equity to give a VP? Series A 0.3–0.8%, by role](https://founderdecisions.com/library/equity/executive-grants-by-stage/): A Series A VP gets 0.3–0.8% fully diluted (Index), eng and product high, sales and finance low. Seed first sales leader: 0.5–2.0%. True CFO: 0.8–1.5%.
- [Venture debt for startups: when it fits and which terms to fight](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/venture-debt/): Venture debt fits right after an equity round, with 12+ months of runway. Size it near 20–40% of the round (SVB). Fight the draw window, MAC, and covenants.
- [Paid pilot before an annual contract: yes, and how it converts](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/pilot-to-paid-contract/): Yes. Charge for a scoped pilot, sign 2–3 success metrics, and put the annual price and signing date in the pilot paper. SaaStr polls: paid 74%, free 42%.
- [How to sell a startup: buyers, LOI, diligence, close](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/how-to-sell-a-startup/): Build buyer ties a year or two early, get a second bid before you sign an LOI, keep exclusivity near 30 days, and price escrow and earnouts before you agree.
- [How to run a seed fundraising process: list, sequence, close](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/seed-fundraising-process/): Run a seed raise as one short, parallel process. DocSend: pre-seed raises averaged 12 weeks in 2024. Size the ask, filter funds, sequence intros, and close.
- [Seed pitch deck: what investors check slide by slide](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/seed-pitch-deck/): A seed deck has to earn the second meeting. DocSend: VCs spend 3 minutes 44 seconds on a seed deck, and 58% read to the end. Slide order and evidence.
- [Executive coaching vs a founder peer group](https://founderdecisions.com/compare/executive-coaching-vs-founder-peer-group/): Coaching is private 1:1 work on how you lead. A peer group puts founders who have sat in your seat on the decision in front of you. Pick by the constraint.
- [Does usage revenue count as ARR? AI usage in the board pack](https://founderdecisions.com/library/finance/usage-revenue-as-arr/): Only contracted commits count as ARR (Burkland, Stripe). Usage above the commit, pay-as-you-go use, pilots, and credits go on their own labeled lines.
- [SAFE vs priced round: which instrument fits Seed](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/safe-vs-priced-round/): Use a post-money SAFE for most Seed raises (Carta's Q1 2026 pre-seed sample is 93% SAFE). Price the round when a lead wants preferred stock and a board seat.
- [How much equity to give an advisor: grants by stage](https://founderdecisions.com/library/equity/advisor-equity/): Carta medians: 0.21% at pre-seed, 0.12% at seed, 0.05% at Series A. FAST tops out at 1%. Vest monthly over two years with no cliff or a 3-month cliff.

## Optional

- [409A after a priced round: cost, timing, and when to grant again](https://founderdecisions.com/library/equity/409a-after-priced-round/): A priced round ends reliance on your prior 409A. Freeze grants, refresh FMV, board-adopt, then grant. Costs, timelines, and common ≠ preferred.
- [Founder vesting schedule at a priced round: credit and 83(b)](https://founderdecisions.com/library/equity/founder-vesting-83b/): Investors often ask founders to re-vest at Seed or Series A. Ask for time-served credit, no new cliff, and double trigger. File 83(b) within 30 days.
- [How to size an option pool: 10–15%, from your hiring plan](https://founderdecisions.com/library/equity/option-pool-from-hiring-plan/): Size the pool from your hiring plan: list hires before the next round, put a cited grant on each seat, add refresh and a buffer, then check against 10–15%.
- [IP assignment agreement for startups: who signs and when](https://founderdecisions.com/library/equity/ip-assignment/): Every founder, employee, and contractor signs a written IP assignment to the company. Founders sign at formation, hires before day one. Use "hereby assign".
- [QSBS for startup founders: who qualifies after 2025](https://founderdecisions.com/library/equity/qsbs/): Stock acquired after July 4, 2025 can exclude 50% of gain at 3 years, 75% at 4, and 100% at 5, up to $15 million or 10x basis. The asset test is $75 million.
- [How to onboard your first executive hire: the first 90 days](https://founderdecisions.com/library/hiring/onboard-first-executive/): Write the 90-day plan before day one, hand over named decisions, review weekly. McKinsey: 68% of executive transitions stumble on politics, culture, and people.
- [Series A C-suite hiring order: who first](https://founderdecisions.com/library/hiring/series-a-leadership-hiring-sequence/): Hire one exec at a time, bottleneck first. B2B SaaS order after Series A (Majhi Group): Sales, Eng, Marketing, People, CFO. AI and DevTools start with Eng.
- [Startup executive compensation: salary and equity by stage](https://founderdecisions.com/library/hiring/executive-compensation-benchmarks/): Founder-CEOs average $153K cash at seed, $203K at Series A, $216K at B (Kruze). Non-founder C-level hires get ~1% equity; the median VP gets 0.17% (Carta).
- [When to hire a VP of Sales: after 10–20 deals and 2 reps at quota](https://founderdecisions.com/library/hiring/when-to-hire-first-vp-sales/): Close 10–20 paid deals yourself, hire two quota-carrying reps, then hire a VP once both hit quota, usually past $1M–$2M ARR. At seed, hire a first seller.
- [AI SaaS gross margin: inference COGS in the board pack](https://founderdecisions.com/library/finance/ai-gross-margin-inference-cogs/): Classic SaaS gross margin bands assume delivery is almost free. Put production inference, GPU serving, retrieval, AI monitoring, and AI support in COGS. Keep training, dev/test inference, and internal AI tools in R&D or OpEx. Show AI gross margin as its own board line with one written definition.
- [Burn multiple vs Rule of 40](https://founderdecisions.com/library/finance/burn-multiple-vs-rule-of-40/): Use burn multiple early and add Rule of 40 later. Sacks rates under 1× amazing and over 3× bad. Formulas, cited bands, and where each fits the board pack.
- [Customer concentration risk: when one customer is too big](https://founderdecisions.com/library/finance/customer-concentration/): One customer above 20% of revenue draws a detailed buyer review. Above 30%, some buyers walk (FOCUS). Measure at the parent level and show the trend.
- [Default alive or default dead: the test, the math, the fix](https://founderdecisions.com/library/finance/default-alive-default-dead/): Default alive means you reach profit on the cash you have at current growth. At 12 months of runway that takes about 6.8% monthly revenue growth.
- [Fractional vs full-time CFO](https://founderdecisions.com/library/finance/fractional-vs-full-time-cfo/): Most Series A startups need CFO-level work before a full-time CFO. Start fractional. Hire full-time when the work is constant or complexity is daily.
- [NRR vs GRR for the board pack](https://founderdecisions.com/library/finance/nrr-vs-grr-board-pack/): Put NRR and GRR on the same board slide. GRR caps at 100% and ignores expansion. NRR can pass 100% and hide a leaky base. Formulas and cited bands inside.
- [How to run a Series A board meeting](https://founderdecisions.com/library/board/series-a-board-meeting-agenda/): Hold a Series A board meeting every 6–8 weeks for 2–3 hours. Send the pack 48 hours to 7 days ahead, put the hard decision first, and end in closed session.
- [How to tell the board you missed the plan: the reforecast](https://founderdecisions.com/library/board/missed-plan-reforecast/): Call each director first, split the gap into timing and lost revenue, and bring a beatable reforecast with the cash plan. Median SaaS: 26% growth vs a 35% plan.
- [Monthly investor update template: what to send between meetings](https://founderdecisions.com/library/board/monthly-investor-update/): Send a short monthly email in a fixed order: TL;DR, the same KPIs every month, highlights, lowlights with the fix, asks, cash/burn/runway, shout-outs. Put it in the body. Monthly is the dominant cadence. Not a substitute for the board pack.
- [When to add an independent board director: Series A, under 1%](https://founderdecisions.com/library/board/when-to-add-independent-director/): Add an independent director at Series A. Carta median grants: 0.50% at seed, 0.40% at Series A, 0.30% at Series B. Pick an operator a stage or two ahead.
- [AEO vs SEO for B2B SaaS startups](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/aeo-vs-seo-b2b-saas/): Split effort between classic SEO and answer engines. Google still calls it SEO. Prioritize bottom-funnel pages; make category answers citation-ready.
- [AI SDR vs a human SDR](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/ai-sdr-vs-human/): An AI SDR does not learn who buys, and it does not close. Put it on research and drafts. Score it on quota, ramp, and pipeline, not meetings.
- [AI code review bottleneck: how to clear the review queue](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/ai-code-review-bottleneck/): Agentic AI pull requests wait 17.6 hours for a reviewer vs 3.4 for human code (LinearB 2026). Cap PR size, name a human owner, tier review by risk.
- [Customer development as your early sales funnel](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/customer-development-sales-funnel/): Run discovery calls as the top of your pipeline. End each stage on something the buyer gives you, write down what makes an opportunity qualified, and judge close rate on qualified deals instead of interviews.
- [DIY founder PR: own the narrative before you hire an agency](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/diy-founder-pr-narrative/): Build the founder narrative first. Pitch as the CEO. Hire a retainer only when you have real news cadence and a list of reporters who already reply.
- [How to build a data moat for an AI startup: what compounds](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/data-moat-ai-startup/): A data moat is a closed loop: scarce inputs, outcome-linked feedback, and a product lift you can measure. a16z found most data network effects erode with scale.
- [How to keep your first customers: feedback and early retention](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/early-customer-retention/): After the first sale, log each request as the task the customer could not finish, give it an owner and an update date, tell the customer what waits and why, and review retention beside new sales before you add more customers.
- [How to market an AI product without leading with AI](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/market-ai-product-without-saying-ai/): AI is the mechanism, not the offer. Lead with the job, the economic outcome, and proof. Score the homepage the way a buyer does.
- [How to negotiate your first customer contract](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/first-customer-contract/): Put the full list price on the order form and show the discount as its own dated line. Trade each point of discount for term, prepay, usage, or a case study, and give on term length before price.
- [How to price AI features to cover inference cost](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/price-ai-features-inference-cost/): Cost sets the floor and value sets the price. Measure AI cost per unit for the typical user and the heavy cohort, include an allowance sized to typical use, and meter the rest with credits or overage priced at or above the floor.
- [How to turn a conference into customers](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/conference-to-customers/): Decide what the event should produce before you buy a ticket, book meetings with named accounts before you fly, end each conversation on a dated next step, follow up within a day, and count qualified opportunities against the full cost.
- [How to validate demand before you build](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/validate-demand-before-you-build/): Write the demand bet as a number, interview buyers about what they did last time, and ask for time, an intro, or money before you write production code. Set the kill line first.
- [How to write a startup landing page that converts](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/landing-page-that-converts/): Name the buyer, lead with the pain, and put the payoff on the button. Then show proof and the product before the price, keep one call to action, and measure qualified demos by traffic source.
- [Repeatable sales process: how to tell before you hire reps](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/repeatable-sales-process/): Your sales process is repeatable when one buyer type buys for one reason, new customers reach value on time, and a non-founder closes. Then hire at a set pace.
- [SOC 2 for startups: when to start, cost, and auditor choice](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/soc-2-for-startups/): Start SOC 2 when an enterprise deal enters the pipeline. Security only, Type 2 in about 4–6 months, $7,500–$20,000 audit fee, about $25,000 all in year one.
- [Seat vs usage vs outcome pricing for AI SaaS (2026)](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/seat-vs-usage-vs-outcome-pricing-ai-saas/): Seats fit copilots, usage fits tokens, outcomes fit agents that close the loop. Bessemer's early hybrid: platform fee at 2× delivery cost plus outcome credits.
- [Selling to the enterprise as an early-stage startup](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/sell-to-enterprise/): Sell to the enterprise when one workflow beats the incumbent by a wide margin and you can deliver within 90 days. Map the champion and budget owner, and send security proof before anyone asks.
- [Should an AI startup file patents? Eligibility, cost, timing](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/ai-startup-patents-ip/): Patent only if your edge changes how the model runs. Generic ML on new data is ineligible. File a provisional pre-pitch. Median fees: $4,500 then $12,000.
- [Startup pricing and packaging: tiers, anchors, first increase](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/startup-pricing-packaging/): Price from buyer value: YC suggests 25–50% of it. Ship two or three tiers, a premium 50–100% above base, discounts that expire, and raise at renewal.
- [Strategic partnerships and IP: what to settle before you sign](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/strategic-partnership-ip/): Before you sign with a big partner: name the deal, give new IP one owner, strike the NDA residuals clause, and keep a sale open: limit exclusivity and ROFRs.
- [Why coding agents don't 10x the company: Amdahl's law on delivery](https://founderdecisions.com/library/gtm/coding-agents-dont-10x-engineering/): Speeding coding alone does not 10x delivery. Score design, code, review, test, deploy, and operate. Invest where the queue sits.
- [Delaware flip for non-US startups: when, cost, and tax](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/delaware-flip/): A Delaware flip makes a new Delaware C corp your parent. Flip before value builds: US-side legal runs $10,000–$14,500 and a full flip takes 12–14 weeks.
- [First angel check: how to find and close early angels](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/first-angel-check/): How to find angel investors for the first check. Warm intros and active writers. Post-money SAFE default. Prefer angels for product capital without a board.
- [How seed VCs decide: what investors check before they invest](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/how-seed-vcs-decide/): Seed VCs decide on the team first, then the market and the evidence. In a survey of 885 VCs, 53% of early-stage firms ranked the team the most important factor, and they close about 1 deal for every 119 they consider.
- [Pre-money vs post-money valuation: the math founders miss](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/pre-money-vs-post-money/): Post-money is pre-money plus new money; the investor owns check ÷ post-money. A 10% pool in the pre-money cuts a $12M headline to $10.5M for existing holders.
- [Quality of earnings report for a startup sale: what to fix](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/quality-of-earnings/): A QoE rebuilds your earnings and ARR from the ledger. QoE gaps sank 21.3% of failed 2025 LOIs on Axial. Fix revenue, add-backs, and working capital first.
- [Raising from US VCs as a foreign founder: when and how](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/us-fundraising-non-us-founders/): Raise from US VCs once you are selling in the US. Only 10% of VC deals start as cold inbound. Expect a Delaware flip ask or PFIC questions from the fund.
- [SAFE and seed round terms: caps, discounts, and valuations](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/safe-seed-terms-benchmarks/): Median post-money SAFE caps ran from $8M for rounds under $250K to $40M at $2.5M+ in Q3 2026 (Carta). 75% of 2026 SAFEs had a cap and no discount.
- [Series A diligence checklist: data room before the term sheet](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/series-a-diligence-checklist/): Build the Series A data room before the term sheet. Finish YC's legal checklist before you sign, and keep every number the same in deck, model, and board pack.
- [Term sheet red flags: which VC terms to push back on](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/term-sheet-red-flags/): Clean VC terms are a 1x nonparticipating preference, broad-based anti-dilution, a board where common holds the majority, and 30–45 days of exclusivity. Push back on anything heavier.
- [US subsidiary for a foreign startup: setup, cost, filings](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/us-subsidiary-setup/): Form a Delaware subsidiary once you build a US team; an EOR covers the first few hires. Index puts setup at $1,000–$2,000. Missing Form 5472 costs $25,000.
- [When runway runs out: diagnose, cut, raise, or land soft](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/when-runway-runs-out/): What to do when startup runway runs short. Snapshot vs trajectory, default alive or dead, cut burn, bridge or angel capital, revenue extension, or orderly wind-down.
- [When to raise Series A: readiness signals that beat the calendar](https://founderdecisions.com/library/fundraising/when-to-raise-series-a/): Raise Series A when you can show a repeatable growth engine. Carta's median seed-to-A wait was about 20 months in Q2 2025. Score ARR, NRR, and runway first.
