You have a buyer ready to sign and a first order form to write. Put your full list price on it, show the discount as a separate line with an end date, and write down what the discount buys: a longer term, annual prepay, a usage minimum, or a case study. If the buyer pushes on price, shorten the term and keep the rate. Take the standard paper on the rest. In Common Paper’s 2026 benchmark of **16,140** signed agreements, **87%** of SaaS contracts auto-renew and Net 30 is the norm for invoiced deals. Only **13%** still carry an automatic fee increase, so a visible discount with an end date is how you get back to list at renewal.

:::takeaways
- Write list, discount, and net as three lines. Innovation Works tells founders to show list and discount on invoices so later increases start from list. Expect a champion to ask for **10–20%** and procurement for about **10%** more.
- Trade the discount for proof. **49%** of Common Paper design partner agreements include a discount on a future subscription, and **20%** is the most common level. SaaStr suggests **30–50%** for **12–24** months for design partners, then standard pricing.
- Hold the price and move the term. A shorter first term answers a lowball without resetting your number (VEN session).
- Plan the renewal now. Automatic fee increases fell from **23%** of Common Paper contracts in 2024 to **13%** in 2026. A dated discount returns the customer to list without an escalator clause.
- Keep multi-year discounts near **10–20%** (Jason Lemkin). Past about **20%**, you give away years of revenue if churn is low.
:::

This page covers the first paid order form. If the buyer wants proof first, start with [how to convert a pilot to a paid contract](/library/gtm/pilot-to-paid-contract/) and come back here for the paper that follows it.

## Write the order form at full price

:::highlight
The list price anchors each renewal and the next customer’s quote. The discount is a line with an end date.
:::

| Order form line | What to write | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| List price | Your rate × units, at full price | Future increases start from list (Innovation Works) |
| Discount | Percent and dollars off list, on its own line | The champion can show the win to their boss |
| Discount end | A date or the first renewal | Lemkin gave his first big customer a decent discount and matched current pricing within **2** years |
| Net price | List minus discount | The number the buyer pays this term |
| What the discount buys | Term, prepay, usage minimum, case study, references | A discount with nothing attached is a price cut |
| Term and renewal | 12 months, auto-renew, 30-day notice | Common Paper 2026: **87%** auto-renew; 30-day notice in about **70%** |
| Payment | Annual invoice, Net 30 | Net 30 is the norm for invoiced deals (Common Paper) |
| Usage | Included units, overage rate, an alert before the cap | Flat price on unlimited use breaks on heavy users |

Common Paper publishes its Cloud Service Agreement as a free standard, with the commercial terms on a cover page. Starting from a standard the buyer’s counsel has seen shortens the redline.

## Same deal, two order forms

:::highlight
Editorial example with round numbers. Not a company and not a benchmark.
:::

A buyer agrees to **$45,000** for year one. Your list price for that scope is **$60,000**.

| Line | Discount hidden in the price | Discount shown and dated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price on paper | $45,000 | $60,000 list |
| Discount line | None | 25% ($15,000), first term only |
| Year one cash | $45,000 | $45,000 |
| Renewal anchor | $45,000 | $60,000 |
| Renewal with a 5% cap | $47,250 | Up to $60,000, or a smaller second-year discount |
| Next customer’s reference price | $45,000 | $60,000 list |

Year one cash is the same. The second column keeps **$60,000** as the price later conversations start from. If the customer uses the product and wants a softer step, you can offer a second-year discount as a new trade.

## Decide what to give and what to hold

:::highlight
Give on terms that cost you little now. Hold the price, the scope, and the liability cap.
:::

| Term | Give | Refuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | A dated discount line, traded for something | A lower list price on the paper |
| Term length | A shorter first term when the buyer lowballs | A lower rate |
| Multi-year | **10–20%** for a prepaid 2- or 3-year commit (Lemkin) | Discounts past about **20%** if churn is low |
| Payment | Net 30 from invoice; quarterly billing if annual cash blocks the deal | Net 90 on a small first contract |
| Renewal price | A written cap, or a stated return to list | A fee freeze past the first term |
| Auto-renewal | A 30-day notice window | Dropping auto-renewal |
| Liability | Cap at **1×** fees, the choice in **96%** of Common Paper contracts | Unlimited liability on general claims |
| Training on customer data | A no-training clause if your product does not need the data | Data rights you cannot explain to their security team |
| Marketing rights | Approval over the case study wording | Dropping the case study the discount paid for |

Most of the give column costs you cash timing or options later. The refuse column protects the number your next ten customers see.

Answer a lowball on duration. Take a lower total for a shorter first term and keep the rate. The renewal clock then puts pressure on the buyer, and the price you renew from is still list. That is judgment from a [VEN session](https://www.venexecs.com/?utm_source=founderdecisions&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=library), not a survey.

## Size the discount and tie it to proof

:::highlight
Size the discount to what you get back. A design partner earns more than a buyer who signs and goes quiet.
:::

**Expect two asks.** Jim Wrubel’s Innovation Works guide tells founders to be ready to discount twice: **10–20%** for the champion, whose performance review may credit the discount, and about **10%** for a purchasing team bonused on savings. Set list so both asks still clear your floor.

**Know what buyers bring.** Vendr CEO Ryan Neu reported an average discount of **28.3%** on contracts under **$50k** and **30.9%** from **$50k** to **$100k**, across **12,525** Vendr contracts in fiscal 2024 and 2025. Those are buyer-side results across all vendor sizes. There is no reliable public benchmark yet for first-contract discounts at Seed to Series A startups.

**Price design partners as a trade.** Common Paper’s 2026 data shows design partner fees in **47%** of agreements, up from **34%** in 2024. **69%** run six months or less. Customers are pushing back on marketing obligations and on feedback sessions, which fell from **80%** to **73%** of agreements. Write the case study, the reference call, and the feedback cadence into the contract before you agree to the discount.

**Cap the depth.** SaaStr warns against over-discounting design partners and suggests **3–5** of them at most. Lemkin’s view on his own first big customer: if you give too crazy a discount, the deal is not real.

The VEN session view on what to trade for: a usage minimum, one consolidated feedback channel on a fixed cadence, before-and-after KPIs agreed at signing, and a pre-approved case study. Investors read usage and referenceable customers as the evidence. Revenue from a customer who does not use the product reads as a warning.

## Set payment, term, and renewal

:::highlight
Take the market’s default term, payment, and renewal mechanics. Spend your negotiating time on price and proof.
:::

**Term.** Default to 12 months. SaaS Capital’s 2025 survey of more than **1,000** private B2B SaaS companies found multi-year contracts at **103%** median NRR and **94%** GRR, against **101%** and **90%** for annual contracts and **100%** and **89%** for month-to-month. Multi-year paper buys a few retention points. Low usage will still show up at renewal. For the metric definitions, see [NRR vs GRR for the board pack](/library/finance/nrr-vs-grr-board-pack/).

**Payment.** Ask for the year invoiced upfront. SaaS Capital found billing frequency had little effect on median growth for a fourth year, and notes the cash flow advantage of annual billing. Innovation Works says most enterprises pay Net 30, some pay Net 90, and many still pay by check. Build the gap between signature and cash into your runway.

**Renewal.** Buyers strike automatic increases more often now. Common Paper saw them fall from **23%** to **13%** of contracts between 2024 and 2026, with custom clauses that freeze fees for the first **24** months or add a money-back guarantee. Large vendors still write them: Vendr reports Salesforce’s standard annual uplift at **9%**, negotiable to **5–7%**. A startup gets further with a dated discount than with an escalator. If you add a cap, put the number in writing.

## Write usage and AI terms before the redline

:::highlight
Usage and data terms are where AI contracts now get stuck. Bring your position to the first call.
:::

**Usage.** If you meter any part of the price, put the included units, the overage rate, and the alert threshold on the order form. Size the allowance with [how to price AI features to cover inference cost](/library/gtm/price-ai-features-inference-cost/), and pick the charge metric first on [seat vs usage vs outcome pricing](/library/gtm/seat-vs-usage-vs-outcome-pricing-ai-saas/). Report usage above the commitment apart from ARR ([usage revenue as ARR](/library/finance/usage-revenue-as-arr/)).

**AI training.** Language about AI appeared in **53%** of Common Paper contracts in the year to June 2026, up from **38%** in 2024. Clauses that prohibit training on customer data rose from under **1%** in 2024 to **14%**. Clauses that grant the vendor training rights stayed near **3%**. Decide what data you need before security asks.

**Liability and extras.** **10.2%** of Common Paper contracts now carry unlimited claims, up from **1.1%** in 2025, covering cases such as gross negligence, willful misconduct, and fraud. SLAs appear in **26%** and insurance requirements in **13%**. Treat an SLA or insurance ask as normal for enterprise buyers, and price the support it implies.

## Paper the first contract in order

:::highlight
Ten steps from list price to signature. Run them before the buyer sends their paper.
:::

:::steps First customer contract
1. **Set list price before the paper conversation.** Anchor it to what the problem costs the buyer today (VEN session).
2. **Write your floor and walk-away.** Know the lowest net price and shortest term you accept.
3. **Start from a standard agreement.** A known MSA and order form shorten the buyer’s legal review.
4. **Write list, discount, and net.** Three lines. Add the discount end date.
5. **Name what the discount buys.** Term, prepay, usage minimum, feedback cadence, case study, references.
6. **Set term and payment.** 12 months, auto-renew with 30-day notice, annual invoice, Net 30.
7. **Write the renewal.** Return to list, a second-year step, or a written cap.
8. **Add usage terms.** Included units, overage rate, alerts, and admin budgets.
9. **Answer a lowball with term.** Shorter first term, same rate.
10. **Get counsel’s review and sign.** Log each concession so the next deal starts from the same list.
:::

## Mistakes that cost the next deal

:::highlight
Each one shows up at renewal or in the next customer’s first call.
:::

**Writing the discounted number as the price.** The renewal and the next reference price start from it.

**A discount with no end date.** The customer reads it as the price.

**Discounting for nothing.** If the case study is not in the contract, plan as if you will not get it.

**Unlimited usage at a flat price.** The heaviest user sets your cost.

**Multi-year discounts past about 20%.** Lemkin: you lock in a decade of discounts if churn is low.

**Accepting unlimited liability to close.** **96%** of Common Paper contracts cap general liability at **1×** fees.

Not legal advice. Have counsel review the order form and the MSA before you sign.
