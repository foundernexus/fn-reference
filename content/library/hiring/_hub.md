Hire the one function that is bottlenecking the next 18 months. Then hire the next. Sources disagree on whether the first seat is Sales or Engineering. They agree you do not staff four VPs in one quarter. True executive seats typically arrive at Series A. Seed is usually still founder-led product and engineering. Not legal, tax, or compensation advice.

:::takeaways
- Index: Series A is when first customer-facing roles and initial executive hires typically arrive. Expect ~4 executives (3 VP + 1 C-suite) as you scale toward B.
- Majhi (B2B SaaS): Sales months 1–4, Eng 3–8, Marketing 6–12, People 9–18, CFO 12–24 or pre-B. Sector tables flip the first seat.
- Nxt Level: trigger is founder time, not the round. First VP when you spend 50%+ of the week on one function.
- Hire for the phase. Say it in the offer. A FounderNexus session put the same split on tenure: the 0→10 operator is often not the 10→50 operator.
:::

## When the first executive is real

:::highlight
Seed builds product. Series A adds go-to-market and the first true exec seats.
:::

Index Ventures ([Rewarding Talent](https://www.indexventures.com/rewarding-talent/)): seed headcount is roughly 0–10, mainly product and engineering. Series A is typically 10–60 people. That is when you ramp engineering and product, add first customer-facing roles, and make initial executive-level hires. Series B shifts to commercial build-out, central teams, and multi-layered management.

Following Series A, Index expects roughly four executives: three at VP level and one at C-suite. Toward Series C+, a team of 6–10 VP-level executives is typical. That is a count through the next chapters, not a 90-day staffing plan.

Nxt Level: the round alone is not the trigger. Hire the first VP when the founder spends 50%+ of the week on one function. If that week is still board, fundraising, and key hires, you may not be bottlenecked yet.

## Composite sequence (cited, not averaged)

| Step | What to do | Who says so |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Name the bottleneck | Hire the single most acute constraint first. Sequential, not simultaneous. | Ready Set Exec; Majhi (do not hire four VPs in one quarter at ~30 people) |
| 2. Check founder time | First VP when you spend 50%+ of the week on one function. | Nxt Level |
| 3. Confirm stage | True exec seats typically at Series A. Avoid title inflation at seed. | Index (Seedcamp / Reshma Sohoni quoted there) |
| 4. If commercial is the bottleneck | Founder-closed deals → two sellers at quota → leader who still sells. Then use Majhi’s Sales months 1–4 for B2B SaaS. | CRO Report; SaaStr; Majhi B2B SaaS |
| 5. If technical is the bottleneck | Eng/Product first. Majhi’s AI/ML and DevTools rows flip the ladder. Nxt Level’s Series A default is often Eng or Head of Product. | Majhi sector table; Nxt Level |
| 6. Finance later | Series A needs CFO-level work. Most shops do not need a full-time CFO yet. Fractional or part-time first. | Kruze |
| 7. Write the grant against pool | Every exec grant is a draw on unallocated pool. Size bottoms-up through the next round. | Index; Carta; sibling equity pages below |

Empty cells are intentional. These rows are different publishers. Do not blend Majhi’s month bands with Index’s headcount table into one calendar.

For classic B2B SaaS after a growth-funded Series A, Majhi’s labeled sketch is: commercial (1–4), technical (3–8), marketing (6–12), people (9–18), CFO (12–24 or pre-B). Flip step 4 and 5 if your sector table says Eng first.

## Equity is part of the hire

:::highlight
Cash and equity come from different publishers. Show each. Do not stack Series B cash on a Series A builder job.
:::

Index: VP grants typically 0.3–0.8% FDE at Series A (product and eng high end; HR and finance lower; VP Sales often lower because of commission). Non-founding C-suite often ~0.8–1.5%. Majhi playbook: Series A VP base typically $180k–$260k; equity 0.4%–1.0%. Write the grant on [executive equity grants by stage](/library/equity/executive-grants-by-stage/). Run remaining pool in the [executive equity calculator](/tools/executive-equity-calculator/). ## Hire for the phase

:::highlight
The 0→10 operator is often not the 10→50 operator. Say the phase in the offer.
:::

A [FounderNexus](https://www.foundernexus.com?utm_source=founderdecisions&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=library) session put the same split on tenure: name the phase out loud. The person who builds the function for this chapter may not be the person who scales it after the next step-change. Majhi’s failure data points the same way: hiring a VP who has mainly operated at a larger stage is the most common Series A mis-hire pattern they call out. Nxt Level: over-titling at Series A creates title inflation you regret when you need a real VP later.

Recruiting these seats is a pursuit, not a two-week LinkedIn blast. Budget calendar time. Once they start, plan a short, structured onboarding window with frequent founder contact in the first months. Give real authority, or you hired a senior IC with a VP title (Nxt Level).
