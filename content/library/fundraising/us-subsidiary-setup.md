A US subsidiary is a US corporation, usually a Delaware C corp, that your home-country company owns outright. Your parent company stays where it is, which makes this the no-flip route into the US. Index Ventures says you need a US company to hire your first US employee unless you use an employer of record (EOR), and recommends an EOR while US headcount is under **10**. Form the subsidiary when you start building a US team or need a US company to sign US contracts. Index puts the subsidiary setup at **$1,000–$2,000** in legal fees and initial US operations at about **$10,000**. Then plan for the filings: the subsidiary files IRS Form 5472 every year, and a missed one costs **$25,000** (IRS). Not legal or tax advice.

:::takeaways
- One or two US hires: an EOR employs them for you. Remote and Deel list EOR from **$599** per employee per month (vendor list prices).
- A sales hire who signs contracts for your home company can create a US taxable presence for it, EOR or not. Put that person in the subsidiary (Alphard Law, US–France treaty art. 5).
- A branch keeps everything in the parent: US tax on the parent, a possible branch profits tax, and a foreign-company ownership report to FinCEN within **30** days of registering (Mintz; FinCEN). A US subsidiary is exempt from that report.
- Getting an EIN takes longer from abroad. Without a US office you cannot apply online; fax takes about **4** business days and mail about **4** weeks (IRS Form SS-4 instructions).
- Price every intercompany charge at arm's length from day one (26 U.S.C. § 482). Underpayments from mispricing carry a **20%** penalty, **40%** for gross misstatements (§ 6662).
:::

This page covers the choice between an EOR, a subsidiary, and a branch, the setup steps, the cost, and the yearly filings. If a US investor wants your parent company to be American, read [Delaware flip for non-US startups](/library/fundraising/delaware-flip/) first. There is no reliable public benchmark yet for the yearly running cost of a US subsidiary (accounting, tax returns, transfer pricing) at Seed to Series A. Law firm and vendor figures below are each firm's own quote.

## EOR, subsidiary, or branch?

:::highlight
Hire your first one or two Americans through an EOR. Form the subsidiary before your first US seller with signing authority, or before US headcount nears 10.
:::

| Your situation | Usual move | What to watch | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First US hire or two, engineering or support, no US company yet | EOR | The EOR is the legal employer; you still direct the work and own the IP terms | Index Ventures; Remote; Deel |
| First US seller who will negotiate and sign customer contracts | Subsidiary first, then hire | A dependent agent who habitually concludes contracts creates a permanent establishment for the parent | Alphard Law (21 October 2026) |
| Building a US team of 5 to 200 employees | Subsidiary plus a PEO for payroll and benefits | The PEO co-employs; your subsidiary stays the legal employer | Index Ventures |
| More than 200 employees in the US | Subsidiary with its own HR, or an ASO provider | Index suggests looking at an ASO above 200 | Index Ventures |
| Modest US sales, no US hires, no US investors | A branch, or no US presence yet | Branch profits tax, possible loss of treaty benefits, FinCEN report | Mintz (26 August 2024); FinCEN |
| US lead investor wants a US parent | Delaware flip | Home-country tax on the share swap | [Delaware flip](/library/fundraising/delaware-flip/) |

Mintz lists four reasons foreign companies choose a subsidiary over a branch: it keeps US liabilities at the subsidiary level, it is more tax-efficient, it eases moving foreign workers to the US, and it makes US bank accounts easier to open. Alphard Law adds the treaty point: under the US–France treaty, owning a US subsidiary does not by itself make the parent a permanent establishment in the US (art. 5, § 7). Check whether your country's treaty with the US has the same clause.

In early November 2026, VEN ran an advisor session with Daniel Glazer, US expansion lead at Wilson Sonsini, on when to set up a US subsidiary and whether the team needs to move.

On fees alone, the subsidiary is cheaper from about the second hire in year one ($10,000 ÷ $500 monthly saving ÷ 12 months ≈ 1.7 hires). ## How to set up the subsidiary

1. **Form a Delaware C corporation with your parent as the sole stockholder.** Index notes most companies use a Delaware C corp. You need a registered agent in Delaware.
2. **Get an EIN.** The IRS online tool is only for applicants with a US principal place of business and an SSN or ITIN. Foreign applicants can call **267-941-1099** (international applicants only), fax Form SS-4 (about **4** business days), or mail it (about **4** weeks). Index says a US accountant holding a power of attorney can speed this up.
3. **Open a US bank account.** Index notes you need the EIN first, and suggests a bank with operations in both your home market and the US.
4. **Register in each state where employees work.** Index: once you have employees or an office in a state, you register to do business there and file state income tax returns. In California, that means a Statement and Designation by Foreign Corporation plus a good standing certificate, and the **$800** minimum franchise tax from the second taxable year (Franchise Tax Board).
5. **Set up payroll.** A PEO such as Justworks runs payroll and benefits while your subsidiary stays the employer (Index Ventures).
6. **Adopt a US option sub-plan.** US hires will expect incentive stock options. Index says a US sub-plan needs a 409A valuation every six months or after a material event, at **$2,000–$5,000** each. See [409A after a priced round](/library/equity/409a-after-priced-round/).
7. **Sign the intercompany agreement.** Index says you need a transfer pricing policy from the outset, a short document at first, and a fuller memorandum once global revenue is material.
8. **Put US contracts on US paper.** Index prices employment contracts at **$500–$1,000** and Americanizing commercial contracts at **$1,000–$7,000**.

## Yearly filings and tax

**Form 5472.** A US corporation that is at least **25%** foreign owned is a reporting corporation, and it reports transactions with its foreign parent on Form 5472 with its tax return. The IRS penalty for a missed or late form is **$25,000**. After 90 days from an IRS notice, another **$25,000** accrues for each 30-day period the failure continues (IRS Form 5472 instructions).

**Transfer pricing.** Section 482 lets the IRS reallocate income between related companies to clearly reflect each one's income. Price the services your subsidiary provides to the parent, and any IP license, on arm's-length terms and keep the support on file (Index Ventures). An underpayment from a substantial valuation misstatement draws a **20%** penalty; a gross misstatement draws **40%** (26 U.S.C. § 6662).

**Sales tax.** Index says about half of US states treat SaaS as taxable, and that you typically owe nothing in a state where you have no employees and under **$100,000** in sales. After the 2018 Wayfair decision, states can tax you without a physical presence, so check thresholds with an accountant before you invoice.

**Ownership reporting.** FinCEN's final rule, effective 14 August 2026, exempts companies created in the US from beneficial ownership reporting. A foreign company that registers to do business in a state, as a branch does, is still a reporting company and has **30** calendar days to file.

## Where founders get this wrong

**Letting the first US seller sign through an EOR.** Alphard Law calls a seller with authority to conclude contracts the classic dependent agent case, and says the subsidiary should come before that employment contract.

**Waiting on the EIN.** Applying from abroad rules out the online tool. Start the fax application the day the Delaware company exists.

**Skipping the intercompany agreement.** Without one, the IRS can reallocate income under § 482, and the penalty starts at **20%** of the underpayment.

**Forgetting Form 5472.** The penalty is **$25,000** per failure, before any continuation penalty.

**Granting US hires options from the home plan.** Index quotes a European founder who skipped the US 409A and US plan: "This became a major problem down the line."

**Flipping when a subsidiary would do.** If the need is US hires and US contracts, a subsidiary under your current parent covers it (Index Ventures). Flip when a US investor makes it a condition.
