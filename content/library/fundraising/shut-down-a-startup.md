To shut down a Delaware startup, the board adopts a resolution and a plan of dissolution, and a majority of the outstanding voting stock approves it (DGCL § 275). You then pay Delaware franchise tax through the month the dissolution takes effect, file the final annual report, and file a certificate of dissolution, which costs **$224** for a one-page filing (Delaware Division of Corporations). File IRS Form 966 within **30** days of the resolution. Then pay or reserve for every claim, file final payroll and income tax returns, and only then distribute what is left: creditors first, then SAFE and preferred holders, then common. SimpleClosure says a dissolution done in this order protects founders from personal liability, except for personal guarantees and unpaid wage and tax obligations. Not legal or tax advice.

:::takeaways
- Two votes start it. A board resolution, then a majority of the outstanding stock entitled to vote, plus any separate preferred vote your charter requires (DGCL § 275; Cooley).
- Delaware will not file the certificate until franchise taxes through the effective month are paid and every annual report is filed (DGCL § 277). The filing fee is **$224**.
- Pick the claims path. The long-form § 280 process bars late claims but makes you wait at least **150** days after the last rejection notice before paying stockholders. The § 281(b) path is faster and requires reserves for claims likely to arise within **10** years.
- The IRS wants Form 966 within **30** days of the resolution, a final Form 1120 marked final, and final 941, 940, W-2, and 1099 filings.
- Buy the D&O tail before you cancel the policy. Six years is the common runoff period for private-company D&O (By Design Law).
:::

This page covers the mechanics after the decision to close. If you are still choosing between cutting, bridging, and closing, start with [when runway runs out](/library/fundraising/when-runway-runs-out/). If a buyer wants the team, compare that offer with a wind-down first ([acqui-hire](/library/fundraising/acqui-hire/)). To see who receives what from the cash left at the end, run the numbers in the [cap table waterfall calculator](/tools/cap-table-waterfall/).

## Shutdown options and what each costs

:::highlight
A solvent company with cooperative creditors can usually dissolve. An insolvent one may need an assignment or bankruptcy.
:::

Cooley's Bob Eisenbach lists the formal paths: corporate dissolution, an assignment for the benefit of creditors (ABC), and Chapter 7 or Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Dissolution needs stockholder approval and fits private companies that expect to pay creditors in full, or whose creditors will cooperate. An informal wind-down, where you lay off staff and stop operating without ending the entity, leaves legal loose ends (Cooley).

| Path | Who runs it | Typical time | Typical cost | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dissolution (managed wind-down) | A director and an officer of the company | **2–8** weeks of active work | **$25K–$75K** | SimpleClosure, July 2026 |
| Assignment for the benefit of creditors | An assignee who sells the assets for creditors | **3–6** months | **$75K–$150K** | SimpleClosure, July 2026 |
| Bankruptcy | A court, and in Chapter 7 a trustee | **6–24** months | **$100K–$500K+** | SimpleClosure, July 2026 |
| Informal wind-down | Founders, with no formal end | Open-ended | Unpaid obligations stay open | Cooley |

SimpleClosure sells shutdown services, and its ranges are averages, not a survey. Its time estimates cover the active process. The waiting periods in the Delaware claims process, below, can run longer. There is no reliable public benchmark yet for what a dissolution costs, or how long it takes, by company size and number of creditors.

Shutdowns are common. Carta counted **966** US customers that left its platform through bankruptcy or dissolution in 2024, up from **769** in 2023, and Carta's Peter Walker said that undercounts (TechCrunch, January 2025). In SimpleClosure's first-half 2026 data, the median company closed **2.28** years after its last raise, and non-AI companies stopped with a median of **$13,000** in the bank.

## Approvals and the Delaware filing

:::highlight
Board first, stockholders second, then the tax clearance, then the certificate.
:::

Under DGCL § 275, the board adopts a resolution that dissolution is advisable and sends it to the stockholders. A majority of the outstanding stock entitled to vote must approve. All voting stockholders can also consent in writing with no board action. Cooley notes that preferred stock can add its own voting procedure, so read the protective provisions in your charter before you count votes.

Delaware then needs its money. Section 277 bars dissolution until franchise taxes are paid for the whole calendar month the dissolution takes effect and the final annual franchise tax report is filed. The Division of Corporations asks you to call or email its Franchise Tax Section before you submit.

| Delaware item | Amount | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Certificate of dissolution (§ 275), one page | **$224**; **$9** per extra page | Division of Corporations form, rev. August 2026 |
| Certified copy | **$50** | Same form |
| Annual report, non-exempt domestic corporation | **$50** | Division of Corporations |
| Minimum franchise tax | **$175** (authorized shares method) or **$400** (assumed par value capital method) | Division of Corporations |
| Late annual report penalty | **$200**, plus **1.5%** a month interest on unpaid tax | Division of Corporations |

Letting the state void the charter is not a shutdown. Under § 510, a Delaware charter becomes void after a corporation goes **1** year without paying franchise tax or filing a complete annual report. SimpleClosure says founders who close this way end up with unfiled franchise tax returns, open EINs, and foreign registrations that surface years later in diligence or background checks. Withdraw your registrations in every other state where the company qualified to do business (Cooley).

## The claims process: § 280 or § 281(b)

:::highlight
The long form bars late claims but takes months. The short form is faster and needs larger reserves.
:::

A dissolved Delaware corporation continues for **3** years to settle claims and distribute assets, and for no other business (DGCL § 278). Within that window you choose how to deal with creditors.

| Step | Long form (§§ 280, 281(a)) | Short form (§ 281(b)) | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Notice to claimants | Mail to known claimants; publish once a week for **2** weeks; national paper too if assets are **$10M** or more | No formal notice | DGCL § 280 |
| Claims deadline | At least **60** days after the notice | None | DGCL § 280 |
| Rejecting a claim | Within **90** days of receiving it; claimant has **120** days to sue | Not applicable | DGCL § 280 |
| Reserves | Pay or secure accepted and court-ordered claims | Reserve for known claims and for claims likely to arise within **10** years | DGCL § 281 |
| Paying stockholders | No sooner than **150** days after the last rejection notice | After the plan of distribution provides for claims | DGCL § 281 |
| Late claims | Barred if not presented by the deadline | Not barred | DGCL §§ 280, 282 |

Directors of a company that follows either path are not personally liable to its claimants (§ 281(c)). A stockholder's liability for a later claim is capped at the amount that stockholder received in dissolution (§ 282). Cooley calls the short form often simpler. A properly run long form can bar late claims, which matters when you cannot list every creditor.

**Labeled example (editorial arithmetic).** The board and stockholders approve on day 0, and the certificate is filed on day 14. On the long form, you mail and publish notice on day 20 with a claims deadline of day 80. One claim arrives on day 78 and you reject it on day 120. The earliest stockholder distribution is day 270, about **9** months after the vote. On the short form, you could distribute once the plan reserves for every known and likely claim, but late claimants can still come after the distributions you made.

## Final payroll, WARN, and tax returns

:::highlight
Unpaid payroll taxes can follow the people responsible for paying them. Pay them first.
:::

The IRS says to pay final wages, make the final federal tax deposits, and report employment taxes. If withheld income, Social Security, and Medicare taxes go unpaid, the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty can reach the people responsible for paying them. State final-pay deadlines vary, and some states require payment on the last day (SimpleClosure).

The federal WARN Act covers employers with **100** or more employees and requires **60** days' written notice before a plant closing or mass layoff (29 U.S.C. §§ 2101–2102). California's version applies at **75** employees, also with **60** days' notice (California EDD). Check your own state's rules as well.

| Filing | When | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form 966, corporate dissolution or liquidation | Within **30** days after the resolution or plan is adopted | IRS Form 966 |
| Final Form 1120, "final return" box checked | By the 15th day of the **4th** month after the date the corporation dissolved | IRS Form 1120 instructions |
| Final Form 941 or 944 | For the quarter of the last wage payment; enter the final wage date (line **17** on Form 941) and name who keeps payroll records | IRS, Closing a business |
| Final Form 940 | For the calendar year of final wages; check box d | IRS, Closing a business |
| Forms W-2 and W-3 | W-2s to employees by the due date of the final 941 or 944 | IRS, Closing a business |
| Forms 1099-NEC and 1096 | For contractors paid in the closing year | IRS, Closing a business |
| EIN cancellation letter | After every return is filed and the tax is paid | IRS, Closing a business |

Keep employment tax records for at least **4** years (IRS). Terminate the 401(k) and any other benefit plan on its own schedule, and file state income, sales, and payroll closing returns in every state where you had employees or nexus.

## Investors, contracts, IP, and insurance

:::highlight
Creditors get paid before any investor. Investors get paid in the order they signed for.
:::

Distributions follow the stack. Creditors come first. A post-money SAFE treats a dissolution like a sale: the holder is owed its purchase amount, junior to creditors and convertible notes, on par with preferred stock, and senior to common (Y Combinator post-money SAFE). Preferred holders then take their liquidation preference, and common gets what remains. Non-AI companies in SimpleClosure's data stopped with a median of **$13,000** in the bank, so common often receives nothing.

Tell your lead investors before the board vote, and send all stockholders the consent with the plan of dissolution attached. Send creditors the § 280 notice if you take the long form. If you owe a bank or venture lender, its right to foreclose can decide how the wind-down runs, so talk to the lender first (Cooley).

**Contracts and leases.** List every contract with notice periods, auto-renewals, and termination fees. The § 281(b) plan has to provide for contingent and unmatured contract claims, which is where leases and multi-year vendor deals land.

**IP and data.** Code, domains, models, data, and hardware are often the last assets with value, and SimpleClosure says they are worth the most while the team can still explain them. Sell them before the team leaves. Customer data carries the promises in your contracts and privacy policy, so delete or return it as those terms require. Check that every founder, employee, and contractor assigned their IP, or a buyer will discount it ([IP assignment](/library/equity/ip-assignment/)).

**D&O tail.** Claims arrive after the company is gone. A tail, also called an extended reporting period, lets former directors and officers report later claims for acts during the policy period. By Design Law says six years is the common runoff period for private-company D&O programs. Bind it while there is cash, because the premium is paid up front.

:::steps Shut down a Delaware startup in order
1. **Freeze spending and build a wind-down budget.** Final payroll, accrued PTO where your state requires it, severance, lease exits, professional fees, the D&O tail, franchise tax, and a claims reserve.
2. **Hire counsel and a CPA.** Pick dissolution, an ABC, or bankruptcy based on whether you can pay creditors in full.
3. **Run the board vote.** Adopt the resolution and the plan of dissolution, and minute the cash position you relied on.
4. **Get stockholder approval.** A majority of the outstanding voting stock, plus any preferred vote in the charter.
5. **File Form 966.** Within **30** days of the resolution.
6. **Clear Delaware.** Pay franchise tax through the effective month, file the final annual report, then file the certificate of dissolution.
7. **Pay final wages and payroll taxes.** Give WARN notice first if you are covered.
8. **Choose the claims path.** Long-form notice under § 280, or a § 281(b) plan with reserves.
9. **Sell assets and end contracts.** IP, domains, and hardware first, then leases and vendors.
10. **File final returns and distribute.** Final 1120, 941, 940, W-2s, and 1099s. Then pay creditors, SAFE and preferred holders, and common in order, and close the EIN.
:::

## Decision table by situation

:::highlight
Match the shutdown path to the cash and the creditor list you have.
:::

| Situation | Do this | Skip this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash covers every known debt and the creditor list is short | Short-form § 281(b) dissolution with a reserve | Publishing notices you do not need |
| Possible unknown claims (product, employment, IP) | Long-form § 280 process | Distributing to stockholders early |
| Cash will not cover creditors | Talk to counsel about an ABC or bankruptcy | A dissolution that pays some creditors and leaves others out |
| A secured lender holds a lien | Negotiate the asset sale with the lender | Moving assets without consent |
| 100 or more employees (75 in California) | 60-day WARN notice | Same-day layoffs |
| A buyer wants the team or the code | Compare the offer with dissolution proceeds | Turning off servers before you sell assets |
| You want to stop paying Delaware | File the certificate of dissolution | Letting the charter go void |
