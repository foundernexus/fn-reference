A Delaware flip is a share exchange. Your shareholders swap their shares in the home-country company for stock in a new Delaware corporation, which becomes the parent. The old company becomes its wholly owned subsidiary (Cooley). Flip when an early US lead or an accelerator makes it a condition: Y Combinator invests only in US, Canadian, Cayman, and Singapore corporations and asks everyone else to flip. If you only need to hire or sell in the US, a Delaware subsidiary under your current parent may be enough (Index Ventures). If you do flip, do it early, while your shares are worth little. Fellow, a San Francisco startup firm, prices the US-side work at **$10,000–$14,500** over **2–4** weeks once documents are ready to sign. Abrams Law puts the whole flip at **12–14** weeks. Not legal or tax advice.

:::takeaways
- In the US, the swap is usually tax-free under § 351 if the people exchanging shares hold at least **80%** of the new parent right after (26 U.S.C. §§ 351, 368(c)).
- Your home country decides the real cost. UK holders can usually roll over with HMRC clearance (Orrick; Wilson Sonsini). Germany treats a flip as a liquidity event (Index Ventures). Canadian holders are taxed on a direct swap unless you use exchangeable shares (Canadian Tax Foundation; Torys).
- Shares you receive in the flip generally are not QSBS. New Delaware stock issued afterward for cash or services can be (Orrick; Wilson Sonsini).
- Flipping back out of the US is close to impossible because of US anti-inversion rules (Orrick; Wilson Sonsini).
- Once you own a foreign subsidiary, the parent files Form 5471 for it. The IRS penalty for missing it starts at **$10,000** per company per year.
:::

This page covers the decision to flip, the tax test in your home country, the mechanics, and the cost. For the US paper you will sign after the flip, use [SAFE vs priced round](/library/fundraising/safe-vs-priced-round/). For the QSBS rules that make Delaware stock attractive to US investors, use [QSBS for startup founders](/library/equity/qsbs/). There is no reliable public benchmark yet for total flip cost or time to close by home country at Seed to Series A. Law firm figures below are each firm's own quote.

## Do you need to flip?

:::highlight
Flip when a US lead or accelerator makes it a condition of the money. If you only need a US team or US contracts, start with a subsidiary.
:::

The pressure comes from the investor, not from US customers. Index Ventures, writing for European founders, says you need a US entity to hire your first US employee (or an employer of record for a few hires). That entity can be a subsidiary of your European parent. Index notes that a US parent "used to be a requirement of US investors" and lists arguments for not rushing.

| Your situation | Usual move | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted to YC, incorporated outside the US, Canada, Cayman, or Singapore | Flip to a parent in one of those four | YC standard deal page |
| US seed or Series A lead asks for Delaware stock | Flip before the round closes, after tax advice in each shareholder country | Wilson Sonsini; Cooley |
| First US hires or US contracts, investors at home | Delaware subsidiary under the current parent | Index Ventures |
| Most customers, leaders, and future investors will be in the US | Flip, and sooner rather than later | Index Ventures (Joe Stolerman) |
| German shareholders and real value in the shares | Get the German tax read before agreeing; the flip may be taxed like a sale | Index Ventures; UmwStG § 1(4), § 21 |

Index also passes on one founder's advice: plan for at least two years with a US parent before a US listing (Pedro Bados, Nexthink). Canada shows how the rules move. YC removed Canada from its list, then put it back on 5 February 2026. Garry Tan wrote that YC had dropped it because its top-performing Canadian companies reincorporated in the US.

The VEN session judgment matches the table: the push to flip is strongest when you need an early US lead. Later-round investors more often accept a strong non-US parent. That is session judgment, not a survey.

## How a flip works

Cooley's checklist for flip transactions starts with tax, then the cap table, then the mechanics.

1. **Form the Delaware corporation.** Wilson Sonsini notes that a Delaware corporation can be formed without issuing any stock. That matters for UK relief, which needs the new parent to issue shares only in the exchange.
2. **Exchange the shares.** With a small cap table, everyone signs a share exchange agreement. With a larger one, Cooley lists drag-along rights in your articles, or a court-sanctioned scheme of arrangement as the least desirable route. The UK has no merger statute, so a contractual exchange is the usual path there (Wilson Sonsini).
3. **Mirror the register.** For UK stamp duty and SEIS/EIS relief, each holder gets Delaware stock with the same rights and in the same proportions as before (Wilson Sonsini).
4. **Replace options and convertibles.** Counsel usually swaps options for options over Delaware stock with the same total value and total exercise price (Wilson Sonsini). Orrick notes that US tax usually treats SAFEs as equity, so decide how each one converts or rolls over before you close.
5. **Clean up contracts and IP.** Cooley lists third-party consents, change-of-control clauses, government grants that restrict IP transfer, and the effect on tax losses.
6. **Paper the group.** Agree intercompany terms at arm's length between the parent and the old company (Cooley). Wilson Sonsini adds transfer pricing and how cash from the next round moves down to the subsidiary.

## Check your home-country tax first

US tax-free treatment does nothing for a shareholder taxed at home. Start with the jurisdiction where your founders and early investors live.

| Home country | What the exchange triggers | What keeps it tax-deferred | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| United Kingdom | Usually no capital gains charge; the Delaware stock takes over your base cost | HMRC clearance (30 days to respond). Clearance is required to keep SEIS/EIS relief; stamp duty of **0.5%** unless relief is claimed within 30 days | Wilson Sonsini; Orrick |
| Germany | Index: flipping is treated as a liquidity event with tax consequences | Share-exchange relief under UmwStG § 21 applies only when the acquiring company is in the EU or EEA (§ 1(4)); a Delaware parent is neither | Index Ventures; UmwStG |
| Canada | A direct swap for Delaware stock is taxed on the gain, even with no cash | Exchangeable shares of a Canadian subsidiary with a section 85 election | Canadian Tax Foundation (Jan 2024); Torys |
| Anywhere else | Varies by country and by shareholder | Advice in each shareholder's country before signing | Cooley; Stripe |

Two UK details catch founders. To keep SEIS/EIS relief, the Delaware parent has to keep its own UK permanent establishment, an office or an agent who signs contracts, until the third anniversary of the last qualifying share issue or of the funded trade starting, whichever is later (Wilson Sonsini). Watch where the board meets too. If the new parent's central management and control sits in the UK, it can become UK tax resident and face an exit charge when that ends (Orrick).

In Canada, Torys adds that Canadian holders lose access to the lifetime capital gains exemption for qualified small business corporation shares once the shares are held in a US corporation.

### Labeled example: why early is cheaper

Editorial arithmetic. Not a company and not tax advice.

A founder paid **$1,000** for her shares. In a country that taxes the swap like a sale, the taxable gain is the value at the flip minus that base cost.

| When you flip | Value of the founder's shares | Taxable gain on the swap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before any outside money | **$1,000** | **$0** |
| After a pre-seed round | **$400,000** | **$399,000** |
| After a seed round | **$3,000,000** | **$2,999,000** |

She receives no cash in any row. The tax is due on shares that have no buyer yet. Torys gives the same advice for Canada: flip as early as possible, before your cap table and operations grow.

## What changes on the US side

**The exchange.** Section 351 lets you transfer property to a corporation for its stock with no gain or loss if, right after, the transferors control it. Control means at least **80%** of the voting power and **80%** of each other class (26 U.S.C. § 368(c)). In a flip your existing holders take 100% of the new parent, so the test is usually met. Orrick adds one wrinkle: if your holders are all non-US and some may move to the US later, a taxable US flip, chosen on purpose, can step up their basis.

**QSBS.** Section 1202 counts only stock issued for money, property other than stock, or services, so the shares you get in the flip generally are not QSBS (Orrick; Wilson Sonsini). New stock the Delaware parent issues afterward for cash or services can qualify. That is the tax benefit your US investors want. See [QSBS for startup founders](/library/equity/qsbs/) for the tests.

**Filings.** The old company is now a foreign subsidiary of a US parent, and the parent files Form 5471 for it each year. The IRS instructions set a **$10,000** penalty per foreign corporation per year for a missed filing. After a notice, another **$10,000** accrues for each 30 days of delay, capped at **$50,000** per failure.

**No way back.** Orrick and Wilson Sonsini both warn that US anti-inversion rules make it very hard to move the parent out of the US later. A reverse flip often leaves the new foreign parent taxed as a US company.

## What it costs and how long it takes

| Item | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US-side legal work for a flip | **$10,000–$14,500**, about **2–4** weeks once documents are ready to sign | Fellow (22 April 2026) |
| End-to-end flip, decision to operating Delaware parent | **12–14** weeks | Abrams Law (21 September 2026) |
| UK HMRC clearance response | **30** days | Wilson Sonsini |
| UK stamp duty without relief | **0.5%** of the shares transferred | Wilson Sonsini |
| US subsidiary only, no flip | **$1,000–$2,000** to set up; about **$10,000** for initial US operations | Index Ventures |
| Delaware franchise tax, every year | Minimum **$175** (authorized shares method) or **$400** (assumed par value capital method); maximum **$200,000**; plus a **$50** annual report fee, due 1 March | Delaware Division of Corporations |
| Late Delaware annual report | **$200** penalty plus **1.5%** interest a month on unpaid tax | Delaware Division of Corporations |

Home-country legal and tax work comes on top of the US-side fee, and no public source breaks it out by country. Abrams names shareholder signatures as the most common cause of delay. Index adds that a US parent based in Europe can slow fundraising paperwork across time zones.

:::steps Flip checklist
1. **Confirm the ask.** Find out which investor wants the flip and whether it is a condition of the lead check or a preference.
2. **Test the subsidiary route.** If the need is US hiring or contracts, price a Delaware subsidiary first (Index Ventures).
3. **Get tax advice in each shareholder country** before you sign anything. In the UK, file for HMRC clearance and allow 30 days (Wilson Sonsini).
4. **Form the Delaware parent with no stock issued** until the exchange (Wilson Sonsini).
5. **Pick the exchange method**: agreement, drag-along, or scheme of arrangement (Cooley).
6. **Map every option, warrant, SAFE, and note** to its Delaware replacement, keeping total value and exercise price the same.
7. **List contracts and grants** with change-of-control or IP-transfer limits, and get consents (Cooley).
8. **Tell founders and early holders** that their flip shares are generally not QSBS (Orrick).
9. **Set up the filings**: Form 5471 for the subsidiary, Delaware franchise tax and annual report by 1 March, and intercompany agreements.
10. **Close the flip before the round**, then issue new Delaware stock to the investors.
:::

## Where founders get this wrong

**Flipping because a US customer asked.** Customers sign with a US subsidiary. Investors are the usual reason to change the parent (Index Ventures).

**Issuing founder stock in the Delaware company early.** For UK holders, that can break SEIS/EIS continuity and stamp duty relief (Wilson Sonsini).

**Reading "tax-free" as worldwide.** Section 351 covers US tax only. Germany and Canada can tax the same swap at home.

**Waiting for the term sheet.** Fellow says to start the conversation before a term sheet is on the table. Every round you raise first adds value and holders to the exchange.

**Assuming you can undo it.** Anti-inversion rules make the reverse move very hard (Orrick).

**Forgetting the annual filings.** Form 5471 penalties start at **$10,000** per company per year, and Delaware charges a **$200** late fee on the annual report.
