A Series A board meeting is a working session you lead, not a sales pitch to your investors. Send a decision-first pack several days ahead. Open with highlights and lowlights. Put the hardest decision early. Batch routine votes into a consent agenda. End with a closed session and named owners. Not legal advice on fiduciary process.

:::takeaways
- Cadence: every 6–8 weeks, about 2–3 hours (Lightspeed / Unusual).
- Pre-read: Feld asks for materials at least 48 hours ahead; CRV aims for 5–7 days on a fuller pack. Assume directors read. Do not re-present the deck.
- Hard item first after a short state-of-the-company open. Updates are not decisions ([FounderNexus session](https://www.foundernexus.com?utm_source=founderdecisions&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=library)).
- Consent agenda for minutes, option grants, and other non-controversial approvals. Pull anything that needs discussion out before the vote.
- Metrics pack belongs in the pre-read: runway, burn, plan vs actual, and retention (see [NRR vs GRR](/library/finance/nrr-vs-grr-board-pack/)). Live time is for judgment calls.
:::

## What Series A changes

:::highlight
CEO owns the agenda, pack, and follow-up until you have real governance support.
:::

At seed and Series A, CRV’s founder guide is blunt: the CEO owns agenda, materials, logistics, and follow-up. Design the pack around decisions. Surface risks. Ask for what you need.

Composition shifts too. CRV: seed boards often have three voting seats (founders plus lead). Series A commonly expands to three to five voting directors (founders, Series A lead, sometimes a continuing seed director). Observers usually appear once you have multiple institutional investors. They attend and stay informed. They do not vote.

Lightspeed’s Series A note (also republished by Unusual): meetings every **6–8 weeks**, duration about **2–3 hours**. Purpose is strategy and plan quality, not a progress report. If you skip the step-back, day-to-day will eat it.

## Pre-read timing and what goes in the pack

:::highlight
The meeting assumes the pack was read. Live time is for decisions and judgment.
:::

| Timing | Owner | What ships |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2–3 weeks out | CEO | Rank decisions by impact. Draft agenda with the ask stated as a decision, not a topic (CRV). |
| 5–7 days out (CRV) or ≥48 hours (Feld) | CEO | Full pack: CEO letter or highlights/lowlights, metrics dashboard, financials, deep-dive memo for the hard item, consent list, draft resolutions. |
| Meeting day | CEO / chair | Timed agenda only. Do not walk slide-by-slide. |
| ≤5 business days after | CEO / secretary | Minutes: decisions, votes, action owners. |

**Pack vs live.** Put trend metrics, plan vs actual, cash and runway, headcount, pipeline snapshot, and prior action-item status in the pre-read. Live: short open, hard decisions, one deep dive, consent vote, closed session.

Feld’s ideal package is thorough and forward-looking, sent several days ahead, ideally commentable so Q&A happens before the room. He prefers not to spend the meeting paging through material directors already had.

## Decision-first agenda (hard item first)

:::highlight
Write the ask as a decision with options. Vague topics burn the clock.
:::

CRV’s fix is concrete. Do not put “Discuss Q2 hiring plan” on the agenda. Put “Approve three engineers in Q2 vs two engineers plus one PM.” Do not put “Review product roadmap.” Put “Build enterprise features for five pilots or ship self-serve to 500 SMBs.”

Operator rule: **updates ≠ decisions**. If the board can only nod, it belongs in the pack or the consent list. If the board’s input changes your choice, it is a live item. Rank by impact and put the hardest live item first after a short open so you do not run out of time on soft updates ([FounderNexus session](https://www.foundernexus.com?utm_source=founderdecisions&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=library)).

Feld’s 2009 bar: most VC boards default to ~80% status / ~10% strategy / ~10% admin. Reverse the status/strategy split. Admin stays small. Feld’s 2014 template: up to five discussion topics that fit on one slide; CEO timeboxes; setup ≤5 minutes per topic, then discussion.

Lightspeed / Unusual open: **10 minutes** of highlights and lowlights in the CEO’s own words. How is the company. How is the market. Top one or two things keeping you up. If the meeting ended there, the board should already have the 80/20.

## Sample timed Series A agenda (≈2.5 hours)

:::highlight
Labeled composite from Feld’s three-block structure plus Lightspeed’s open and CRV’s decision blocks. Adjust to your board.
:::

| Block | Time | What happens | Source frame |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Call to order + consent agenda | 10–15 min | Quorum. Approve prior minutes, option grants, routine resolutions in one vote unless pulled. | Feld admin block; consent practice |
| CEO open (highlights / lowlights) | 10 min | State of company and market. Top 1–2 worries. No slide tour. | Lightspeed / Unusual |
| Pack Q&A (metrics & finance) | 15–20 min | Questions only on pre-read: cash, runway, burn, plan vs actual, retention. | Feld “questions on the package” |
| Hard decision #1 | 25–30 min | Highest-stakes ask first. Options, recommendation, vote or clear next step. | CRV decision-first |
| Hard decision #2 or deep dive | 25–30 min | Second decision, or one functional deep dive (team, pipeline, product, GTM, finance). | Lightspeed deep-dive norm |
| Optional third topic | 15–20 min | Only if still high impact. Else end early. | Feld: ending early is fine |
| Action items | 10 min | Named owners and dates. Capture votes. | CRV next-steps block |
| Closed / executive session | 20–30 min | Directors (+ CEO as appropriate), then directors alone. Comp, CEO feedback, sensitive personnel. | Feld exec session; Lightspeed closed session |

Total lands near Feld’s three-hour ceiling without padding. Shorter is better when the topics are done.

## Consent agenda

:::highlight
Batch the non-controversial. Pull anything that needs airtime before the vote.
:::

A consent agenda groups routine approvals into one motion: prior minutes, standard option grants already reviewed in the pack, administrative resolutions. Any director can pull an item into discussion. What remains passes in one vote. That protects time for the hard decisions.

Put the consent list in the pack with the draft resolutions. Do not invent discussion in the room for items nobody flagged.

## Closed session and minutes

:::highlight
Closed time is for candor. Minutes are the legal record of decisions, not a transcript.
:::

Lightspeed: open session for directors, observers, and executives; closed session for sensitive matters such as compensation. Feld’s ideal: CEO-and-board feedback time, then CEO steps out so directors can confer and the lead director returns feedback.

Minutes should record decisions, votes, and action items with owners. Send them promptly after the meeting. They are not a play-by-play of every comment.

## Series A metrics that belong in the pack

:::highlight
Same dashboard every meeting. Variance and meaning live; raw charts sit in the PDF.
:::

Lightspeed’s five standing areas (overview slides each meeting, deep dive on one): **team**, **sales pipeline**, **product/engineering**, **marketing**, **finance** (12-month operating plan with monthly/quarterly targets). Keep the format stable so directors can track progress.

For finance and efficiency, point directors to sibling pages rather than reinventing formulas in the room:

- Cash, net burn, and months of runway → [runway calculator](/tools/runway-calculator/)
- Efficiency when still burning → [burn multiple vs Rule of 40](/library/finance/burn-multiple-vs-rule-of-40/)
- Retention without masking → [NRR vs GRR for the board pack](/library/finance/nrr-vs-grr-board-pack/)

Observers get the open-session pack and discussion norms you set with counsel and the lead director. They do not vote (CRV). Clarify in advance whether observers stay for any part of closed session. Default is no.

## Mistakes that waste the meeting

:::highlight
Status theater, late packs, and soft asks are the usual failure modes.
:::

**Treating the meeting as a sales deck.** Lightspeed: this is not a sales meeting to the board. Good, bad, and ugly. Experienced directors are not scared by facts. They are stuck if you hide them.

**Sending the pack the night before.** Feld’s 48-hour floor exists so “I didn’t have time to read” is not an excuse. CRV’s 5–7 day window is stricter for heavier packets.

**Walking every slide.** Feld’s favorite meetings barely refer to paper once discussion starts.

**Saving the hard item for the end.** Fatigue and travel clocks kill the decision. Put it first after the open.

**Brainstorming instead of leading.** Lightspeed: deep dives are the CEO leading the plan and metrics, then taking feedback. Not an open jam session.
