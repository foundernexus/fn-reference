Send a short monthly email in a fixed order: TL;DR, the same KPIs every month, highlights, lowlights with the fix, specific asks, cash/burn/runway, shout-outs. Put the substance in the body. An attachment is optional, not required for the story. Visible’s 2026 benchmarks (3.5M+ updates) show monthly as **65%** of sends, ahead of quarterly (**22%**) and weekly (**13%**). The update keeps investors warm between decision-first board meetings. It is not a substitute for the board pack. Not legal, tax, or investment advice.

:::takeaways
- Cadence: monthly for early stage (Warp: pre–Series B). Visible: **65%** monthly, **22%** quarterly, **13%** weekly. Pick a date and stick to it.
- Fixed order: TL;DR → same KPIs every month → highlights → lowlights with the fix → asks → cash/burn/runway → shout-outs. Warp + YC six-section frame; Founder Collective fill-in; Visible block shares (Highlights **23%** most used, Lowlights **10%** least).
- Metrics every month: ARR/MRR + MoM, NRR or churn, cash, burn, runway (Warp). Keep the definition identical to the board pack ([finance hub](/library/finance/)).
- Send habits: CEO sends; body under two minutes (Warp: most great updates **200–400** words plus a metrics table). Visible median open rate **77%**; board **78%**, current investors **77%**, prospects **64%**. Smaller lists open more (**1–5** at **100%**; **50+** at **69%**).
- Updates are not decisions ([FounderNexus session](https://www.foundernexus.com?utm_source=founderdecisions&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=library)). Bad-month consistency matters more than polish. First Round / Paul Arnold: “investors can only help if you are transparent all the time,” including in hard months.
:::

## Why monthly beats ad hoc

:::highlight
Visible: founders on a regular cadence report more engagement. Ad hoc senders report flat.
:::

Visible’s 2026 Investor Update Benchmarks cover **3.5M+** updates. Cadence share: monthly **65%**, quarterly **22%**, weekly **13%**. Among founders who send on a regular cadence, **78%** report increased investor engagement and zero report a decrease. Ad hoc senders report flat engagement. The top-selected benefits of a regular cadence are accountability (**73%**) and investor relations (**60%**).

Warp (Ayush Sharma, 25 Jan 2024, updated July 2026) frames early-stage (pre–Series B) as monthly and later stage as often quarterly. First Round Review / Paul Arnold (published 3 Mar 2015, updated 29 Feb 2024) asks for at least two updates per quarter covering wins, challenges, product, sales/traction, hiring, financials, and needs. That is a floor, not a ceiling, for founders who already ship monthly.

A [FounderNexus session](https://www.foundernexus.com?utm_source=founderdecisions&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=library) made the same operating point: keep the relationship continuous between decision-first board meetings, and do not treat the update as a decision forum. Bad-month consistency matters more than polish.

## Fixed section order

:::highlight
Same sections every month, so investors learn where to look. Asks are the most underused block.
:::

| Section | Purpose | Source frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TL;DR / exec summary | One screen: what changed, what you need | Warp / YC; Founder Collective TL;DR |
| KPIs (same set every month) | Trend without redefining the metric | Warp metrics minimum; Visible KPIs block **14%** of content |
| Highlights | Wins with named evidence | Visible Highlights **23%** (most used); Warp highlights |
| Lowlights + fix | What broke and the next step | Visible Lowlights **10%** (least used); Warp lowlights; Founder Collective “what I am freaked out about” |
| Asks | Specific intros, hires, customers, advice | Warp: most underused; Visible Asks **11%**; Founder Collective “what I need help with”; Arnold wish lists |
| Cash / burn / runway | Liquidity line investors can trust | Warp cash, burn, runway |
| Shout-outs / thank-yous | Name who helped | Warp shout-outs; Founder Collective thank-yous; Arnold thank investors publicly |
| Priorities / what’s next | Near-term focus (optional short block) | Warp priorities; Founder Collective what’s next |

Visible content-block shares on the same report (Highlights **23%**, KPIs **14%**, Fundraising **12%**, Product launches **11%**, Team **11%**, Asks **11%**, Thank you **10%**, Lowlights **10%**). Highlights are the most used block. Lowlights are the least. Warp’s YC-aligned six core sections are exec summary, metrics, highlights, lowlights, shout-outs, and priorities, with asks called out as most underused. Founder Collective / Micah Rosenbloom (28 Sep 2017) adds fundraising status, learnings, team, “what I feel good about,” and “what I am freaked out about” as fill-in prompts inside that same rhythm.

**25%** of Visible updates include a chart; among those with charts, the median is **4** charts. **50%** include an image. Charts help when the KPI table needs a trend line. They do not replace the fixed section order.

## Metrics that belong every month

:::highlight
Warp’s minimum set. Match board-pack definitions so the email and the meeting agree.
:::

| Metric | Why it belongs | Sibling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ARR / MRR + MoM | Top-line trend in one line | [Finance hub](/library/finance/) |
| NRR or churn | Retention without a surprise at the board | [NRR vs GRR](/library/finance/nrr-vs-grr-board-pack/) |
| Cash | Absolute liquidity | [Runway calculator](/tools/runway-calculator/) |
| Burn | Net cash burn for the period | [Burn multiple vs Rule of 40](/library/finance/burn-multiple-vs-rule-of-40/) |
| Runway | Months of cash at current burn | [Runway calculator](/tools/runway-calculator/) |
| Headcount / customers | Scale context beside revenue | Optional; keep definitions stable |

Warp’s minimum is Revenue MRR/ARR + MoM, NRR or churn, cash, burn, and runway. Add headcount or customer count when your board already tracks them. Do not invent a new KPI for a good month and drop it the next. If you change a definition, say so in the TL;DR and restate the prior month on the new rule.

## Cadence, list, and send habits

:::highlight
CEO sends. Smaller lists open more. Prospects belong on a separate list.
:::

Visible open rates by audience: Board **78%**, Current investors **77%**, Team **77%**, Advisors **75%**, Family & friends **71%**, Prospective investors **64%**. Median open rate across the platform is **77%**. Open rate by list size (main “How many recipients” table): **1–5** recipients **100%**; **6–15** **83%**; **16–30** **79%**; **31–50** **77%**; **50+** **69%**.

Send timing on Visible: Monday is **20%** of sends; Friday is **19%**. Friday and Wednesday show the highest median open rates. **26%** of updates land in the first **7** days of the month; **17%** after the **25th**; the **1st** is the most popular send date. Warp: send yourself (CEO), pick a date and stick to it, and send in bad times. Keep the update in the email body, under two minutes to read; most great updates are **200–400** words plus a metrics table.

Put prospective investors on a separate list from current investors and the board. Visible’s prospect open rate (**64%**) sits below current investors (**77%**). Mixing lists blurs who is already on the cap table and who is still in process. Y Combinator’s Investor Update template is the most-used template on Visible.

```
Subject: [Company] investor update: [Month Year]

TL;DR
- [One sentence on the month]
- [One sentence on the ask or the risk]

KPIs (same definitions every month)
| Metric | This month | MoM | Notes |
| ARR / MRR | [ ] | [ ] | |
| NRR or churn | [ ] | [ ] | |
| Cash | [ ] | — | |
| Burn | [ ] | [ ] | |
| Runway (months) | [ ] | [ ] | |

Highlights
- [Win + evidence]
- [Win + evidence]

Lowlights + fix
- [What broke] → [Next step / owner / date]

Asks (specific)
- Intro to [named role / company]
- Help on [open role]
- Advice on [decision]

Cash / burn / runway
- Cash: [ ]
- Burn: [ ]
- Runway: [ ] months

```

Fill every bracket with your numbers and names. Do not paste a prior month’s table without refreshing cash, burn, and runway.

## Mistakes that burn trust

:::highlight
Each mistake below trains investors to stop reading or stop helping.
:::

**Skipping bad months.** Warp: it is more important to send in bad times. Arnold: stay transparent in hard months, not only when the chart looks good. Visible’s regular-cadence founders report engagement up; ad hoc senders report flat.

**Hiding lowlights.** Lowlights are the least-used Visible block (**10%**). Put the miss and the fix in the same bullet.

**Vague asks.** “Any intros?” wastes the list. Arnold wants specific named prospects and open roles. Warp flags asks as the most underused section.

**Redefining KPIs month to month.** Investors compare rows. Match the [board pack](/library/board/series-a-board-meeting-agenda/) definitions.

**Attachments as the only substance.** Warp: keep the update in the body. A deck can sit behind a link; the email should stand alone in under two minutes.

**Mixing prospects into the investor list.** Prospect open rates are lower (**64%** vs **77%** for current investors on Visible). Separate lists keep fundraising status honest.

**Treating the update as a board meeting.** Updates are not decisions ([FounderNexus session](https://www.foundernexus.com?utm_source=founderdecisions&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=library)). Hard votes and strategy belong in the pack and the room.

## Monthly investor update lock

:::highlight
Nine steps from calendar to send. Run them the same way every month.
:::

:::steps Monthly investor update lock
1. **Pick a permanent send date.** Visible’s **1st** is the most popular day. Warp: stick to it.
2. **Freeze the KPI set.** ARR/MRR + MoM, NRR or churn, cash, burn, runway (Warp). Write definitions once with the [finance hub](/library/finance/).
3. **Draft TL;DR last.** Write highlights, lowlights, and asks first, then compress to two sentences.
4. **Write lowlights with a fix.** Miss + owner + date in one bullet. Do not bury the miss in a soft paragraph.
5. **Make asks specific.** Named intros, open roles, one decision you want advice on (Arnold; Warp).
6. **Separate lists.** Current investors and board on one list; prospects on another (Visible open-rate gap).
7. **CEO sends from the body.** Under two minutes; **200–400** words plus the metrics table (Warp). Optional chart or image (Visible: **25%** include a chart; **50%** an image).
8. **Send in bad months too.** Regular cadence shows engagement up (**78%** of Visible regular senders); ad hoc is flat.
9. **Do not turn the reply thread into a board.** Park decisions for the next [Series A board meeting](/library/board/series-a-board-meeting-agenda/).
:::

## Monthly email vs the board pack

:::highlight
The email keeps people informed. The pack and the room make decisions.
:::

Board meetings run every **6–8** weeks for about **2–3** hours on a decision-first pack ([how to run a Series A board meeting](/library/board/series-a-board-meeting-agenda/)). The monthly update sits between those meetings. Same KPI definitions, shorter form, no consent agenda, no closed session. A [FounderNexus session](https://www.foundernexus.com?utm_source=founderdecisions&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=library) put it as: updates are not decisions. Use the email to keep the relationship continuous. Use the board pack when you need a vote, a hire, a raise posture, or a strategy call.
